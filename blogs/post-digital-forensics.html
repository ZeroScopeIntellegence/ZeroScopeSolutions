Digital Forensics Investigation

Every attacker leaves artifacts. Learn how investigators collect, preserve, and analyze digital evidence to reconstruct what happened — and who did it.

Published November 3, 2025 · Author Wil Dennison · Read time 7 min read

As technology continues to advance, organizations generate and rely on massive amounts of data every day. With this growth comes an increased risk of cyberattacks, making it more important than ever to detect, investigate, and respond to security incidents quickly. Digital forensics provides a structured approach to uncovering and analyzing digital evidence after an attack — helping teams determine what happened, how it happened, and how to prevent it from happening again.

Digital forensics is a specialized branch of forensic science that focuses on examining data from systems, endpoints, user activity, and other digital sources to identify, respond to, and prevent security breaches or insider threats. It also helps investigators understand who may be responsible for an attack and the techniques they employed.

The Investigation Process

  1. Identification Potential sources of evidence — computers, mobile devices, cloud storage, and networks — are located and documented to define the scope of the case. This stage sets the boundaries of the investigation.
  2. Collection A forensic image is created — an exact bit-by-bit copy of the data. This ensures the original evidence remains completely untouched during analysis. Chain of custody documentation begins here to ensure evidence integrity.
  3. Analysis The collected data is carefully examined to uncover relevant evidence. Investigators use specialized tools and methods to recover deleted files, decrypt data, and analyze system logs — ensuring no detail is overlooked.
  4. Reporting Investigators produce a formal report summarizing the methods used, findings uncovered, and conclusions drawn from the investigation. This document must be thorough and defensible.
  5. Presentation Findings are shared with stakeholders — whether clients, courts, or internal teams. Technical information must often be translated into language that non-experts can understand and act on.

Common Forensic Tools

Autopsy

A modular platform with a graphical interface that helps investigators examine data from computers and mobile devices. Autopsy supports file recovery, keyword searching, timeline analysis, and artifact extraction — making it one of the most widely used open-source forensic platforms.

COFEE

Computer Online Forensic Evidence Extractor — a Microsoft toolkit designed for quick evidence extraction from live Windows systems. COFEE allows first responders to collect volatile data before it disappears when a system is powered down.

Wireshark

A widely used network protocol analyzer that captures and visualizes network traffic across multiple operating systems. In forensic investigations, Wireshark helps reconstruct attacker communications and identify the data that was transmitted during an incident.

// Key Takeaway
Digital forensics enables organizations to detect, analyze, and respond to cyber incidents efficiently. By following a structured process — identification, collection, analysis, reporting, and presentation — investigators can uncover key evidence and communicate findings clearly. Digital forensics is not just about solving incidents after they occur; it strengthens cybersecurity defenses and helps prevent future attacks.
