How Attackers Use Artificial Intelligence to Send Malicious Emails and How Defenders Respond

AI-generated phishing is indistinguishable from legitimate email. Here's how attackers are using it — and how defenders are fighting back.

Published November 18, 2025 · Author Wil Dennison · Read time 7 min read

Phishing remains the most common entry point for modern cyberattacks, and artificial intelligence is now making these attacks more convincing and harder to detect. Attackers increasingly use AI tools to generate, customize, and send malicious emails at a scale and level of realism that traditional security filters struggle to identify. At the same time, cybersecurity defenders have begun adopting AI-powered tools to counter them.

How Attackers Use AI

Generating Convincing Email Content

Historically, phishing emails were easy to identify because they contained spelling mistakes, awkward phrasing, or obvious red flags. Today, AI-generated emails eliminate many of these signs. Attackers use generative AI models to write clean, grammatically correct messages that match the tone of executives, coworkers, banks, or well-known brands.

// Spear-Phishing at Scale
AI can study public data — LinkedIn posts, company websites, and previous email exchanges — to produce targeted phishing emails that appear completely natural. This enables highly effective spear-phishing campaigns tailored to each individual recipient, at a scale no human team could match manually.

Automating Mass Campaigns

AI enables attackers to automate the process of sending malicious emails. Instead of manually writing each message, attackers instruct AI systems to generate thousands of unique emails that contain harmful links or attachments. AI-generated messages automatically vary the subject line, wording, and formatting — helping them avoid detection by filters that look for repeated patterns. This dramatically increases both the reach and the success rate of attacks with minimal effort.

Building Malicious Infrastructure

AI also supports the infrastructure behind phishing campaigns. Attackers use AI to create realistic phishing websites that resemble login portals for services like Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, or online banking. These sites are designed to capture credentials once a victim clicks the link. AI can also:

  • Generate dynamic URLs that change regularly
  • Rotate domains to evade blocklists
  • Adjust web content in real-time to avoid URL-based security tools
  • Create convincing lookalike login pages using visual cloning

How Defenders Use AI

Natural Language Analysis

Modern email security systems use natural language processing (NLP) to examine writing patterns, communication tone, and behavioral clues hidden within incoming messages. These tools identify warning signs such as impersonation attempts, unusual sentence structure, or sudden changes in message intent — even when an email looks completely legitimate.

URL and Attachment Analysis

AI-powered security systems classify links by examining domain age, redirect behavior, and typosquatting traits. Computer vision tools analyze images, QR codes, and email attachments for visual clues that indicate phishing. This goes beyond simple blocklists — AI evaluates context and intent.

Behavioral Detection

Behavioral AI monitors user actions after an email is opened and can detect:

  • Unusual login activity following email interaction
  • Impossible travel patterns — logins from two distant locations within minutes
  • Unfamiliar device or browser access
  • Credential use on previously unseen systems

All of these can signal a compromised account — even if the phishing email itself wasn't caught before delivery.

The Limitations

Although AI improves detection, it is not perfect. Poorly trained models may generate false positives that flag legitimate emails. Advanced attackers continue developing methods to bypass AI detection. There are also concerns that attackers may feed manipulated data into AI systems to weaken their accuracy — a technique known as adversarial machine learning.

Organizations must also balance the power of AI monitoring with privacy considerations, since email scanning involves processing sensitive communication.

// Key Takeaway
AI is reshaping how phishing attacks are created and delivered. Attackers use AI to write convincing emails, personalize messages at scale, and connect victims to intelligent malicious infrastructure. Defenders rely on AI to analyze language, behavior, and web content in ways traditional filters cannot. While AI will not eliminate phishing, organizations that adopt AI-enhanced security tools will be significantly better prepared for the evolving threat landscape.
