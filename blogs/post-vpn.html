VPN (Virtual Private Network)
Published June 2026 · Author Wil Dennison · Read time 5 min read

Whether you are a cybersecurity professional, an IT administrator, or an everyday internet user, understanding VPN technology is essential in today's digital landscape. VPNs help secure communications, protect sensitive data, and enhance privacy by creating encrypted connections between users and the internet.

What is a VPN?

A Virtual Private Network (VPN) is a security technology that creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and a VPN server over the internet. This allows your internet traffic to travel securely and privately while masking your real IP address.

Example of a VPN:

01
The Tunnel:Creates a private path through the internet.
02
The Lock:Encrypts your data so nobody else can read it.
03
The Exit Door:Websites see the VPN server's location instead of your actual location.

Why is a VPN Important for Your Home Internet?

According to Fortinet, a VPN is a valuable tool for securing your internet connection by encrypting your traffic and masking your IP address. It helps protect personal data from hackers on public Wi-Fi networks, reduces the amount of browsing activity your Internet Service Provider (ISP) can collect, and can help bypass regional content restrictions.

Are VPNs Safe?

Yes, VPNs are generally safe to use, but their effectiveness depends on the trustworthiness and reliability of the provider you choose. When you use a VPN, all of your internet traffic is routed through the provider's servers. In a sense, you are transferring some trust from your Internet Service Provider (ISP) to the VPN company. This is why choosing a reputable VPN provider with a strong privacy policy is important.

Common Types of VPNs

Remote Access VPN:

A Remote Access VPN allows a user to connect to a private network from another location securely. This is commonly used by employees working from home to safely access company resources.

Site-to-Site VPN:

A Site-to-Site VPN connects entire networks over the internet. Businesses often use this type of VPN to securely link multiple office locations together.

Cloud VPN:

A Cloud VPN extends a company's network into cloud environments, allowing employees and systems to securely access cloud-based applications and services.

Why do we use VPNS in Cybersecurity?

In cybersecurity, we use VPNs (Virtual Private Networks) primarily to encrypt internet traffic and mask IP addresses. They establish a secure "tunnel" between your device and the network, protecting sensitive data from interception, especially when operating outside the fortified defenses of a traditional office network.

The essential cybersecurity benefits of a VPN include:

  • Public Wi-Fi Protection: Unsecured networks (e.g., in coffee shops, hotels, or airports) are highly vulnerable to "man-in-the-middle" attacks. A VPN encrypts your connection so that even if a cybercriminal intercepts your data, it remains unreadable
  • Secure Remote Access: VPNs allow remote employees to safely connect to the corporate network from anywhere in the world, ensuring confidential business data isn't exposed to the public internet.
  • IP Masking and Privacy: By routing your traffic through a remote VPN server, your device's true IP address is hidden. This prevents ISPs (Internet Service Providers), advertisers, and malicious actors from tracking your physical location and browsing habits.
  • Data Integrity and Confidentiality: VPNs use protocols like IPsec and TLS to transform data into scrambled, secure code, preventing unauthorized access and helping organizations meet regulatory compliance (like HIPAA or GDPR)

Conclusion

The decision to use a VPN comes down to balancing privacy and security with performance. While there can be minor drawbacks, such as slightly reduced connection speeds or occasional website restrictions, the benefits often outweigh the disadvantages. A VPN helps protect your data through encryption, masks your IP address, and provides additional privacy when using public Wi-Fi networks. However, it is important to remember that a VPN is not a complete cybersecurity solution. It cannot prevent malware infections, phishing attacks, or poor security practices. For the best protection, use a reputable VPN provider alongside strong passwords, multi-factor authentication (MFA), software updates, and good cybersecurity practice.
