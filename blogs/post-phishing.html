Phishing Attack Prevention: How to Identify and Avoid Phishing Scams

Phishing is behind over 90% of data breaches — yet most people still can't spot one in time.

Published June 2026 · Author Wil Dennison · Read time 5 min read

In a real-world scenario, you may receive an email that appears to come from a trusted company that your organization regularly does business with. Before taking any action, it is important to verify that the email is legitimate.

Phishing emails often create a sense of urgency in an attempt to pressure recipients into acting quickly. For example, the email may contain warnings such as:

  • "Your account will be suspended."
  • "Unauthorized login detected."
  • "Your password has expired."
  • "Immediate action required."

The email will typically encourage you to click a link or button that directs you to a website.

// Threat Alert
In many phishing scams, the link leads to a fraudulent website designed to look nearly identical to the legitimate site. In some cases, attackers may even redirect you to the actual website while displaying a fake pop-up window designed to collect your sensitive information.

Once on the site, you may be asked to update your account information or verify your identity by providing personal details such as:

  • Social Security number
  • Account number
  • Password
  • Security questions and answers
  • Other personal identifying information

Providing this information can allow attackers to gain unauthorized access to your accounts, steal personal information, or commit financial fraud.

How Can You Protect Yourself from Phishing Attacks?

  1. Never Provide Personal Information in Response to an Unsolicited Request Never provide personal or sensitive information in response to an unsolicited email, phone call, text message, or website request. Phishing emails and websites are often designed to look legitimate. If you did not initiate the communication, do not provide any information.
  2. Contact the Company Directly If you believe the message may be legitimate, contact the company directly using verified contact information from an official website or trusted source. Do not use phone numbers, email addresses, or links provided in the suspicious message.
  3. Never Share Passwords or Verification Codes Legitimate companies will not ask you to provide your password, MFA code, or other sensitive credentials through an unsolicited email. If someone requests this information, treat the communication as suspicious and verify it independently.
  4. Verify Suspicious Links and Attachments Before clicking links or opening attachments, verify that they are safe. Hover over links to inspect their destination and use trusted security tools to analyze suspicious content. When in doubt, contact your IT or security team.

What to Do If You Become a Victim?

  1. Report the Incident Immediately If you believe you have fallen victim to a phishing attack, report the incident to your organization's IT or security team as soon as possible. Early reporting can help contain the threat and protect others from becoming victims.
  2. Change Your Passwords If you believe your account has been compromised, change it immediately. If you use the same password on other accounts, update those as well. Always use strong, unique passwords and enable 2FA or MFA whenever possible.
  3. Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) If MFA is not already enabled on your accounts, activate it immediately. MFA provides an additional layer of protection and can help prevent unauthorized access even if your password has been compromised.
  4. Contact the Company or Organization If you responded to a phishing email that appeared to come from a trusted source, contact the company directly. They may be able to secure your account and prevent others from falling victim to the same scam.
// Key Takeaway
Phishing attacks continue to be one of the most common cybersecurity threats. By staying vigilant, verifying unexpected requests, and following security best practices, you can help protect both your personal information and your organization from cybercriminals.
